Introduction to Channel Management
Channel management is the process of overseeing and optimizing sales relationships between a company and its distribution or sales partners. These partners can include reseller, distributor and referral partner types who help bring products or services to the end customer. A successful Channel Account Manager (CAM) ensures that these partners are aligned with the company’s objectives, are trained and supported, and are motivated to perform at their best.

The foundation of successful channel management is a clear and effective channel strategy. Key steps in creating a channel strategy include:
Define Objectives:
Identify what the company hopes to achieve through its channel partners, e.g., revenue targets, market expansion, or brand awareness.
Segment Partners:
Classify partners based on factors like size, potential, market focus, and capability.
Partner Selection Criteria:
Establish clear criteria for identifying and recruiting high-quality partners.
Channel Programs:
Provide incentive programs, training, and support mechanisms to motivate and enable channel partners.
Communication Plan:
Design an effective communication framework for sharing information, updates, and resources with partners.

Types of AWS Channel Partners
AWS Consulting Partner
offers consulting and professional services to help organizations implement, migrate, and optimize AWS solutions. They are typically system integrators, managed service providers, and consulting firms that have deep expertise in AWS cloud adoption and transformation.
AWS GSI Partner
refers to a
Global Systems Integrator
that has a strategic partnership with Amazon Web Services (AWS) to help clients design, implement, and manage cloud-based solutions using AWS services. These partners typically have deep technical expertise, a global presence, and the resources to assist large enterprises in adopting cloud technologies at scale.
An
AWS Distributor
is an organization that partners with Amazon Web Services (AWS) to resell AWS services and offer additional value-added services, typically through a network of resellers, consultants, and managed service providers (MSPs). AWS Distributors play a key role in helping businesses adopt AWS solutions by facilitating access to AWS products, providing local support, and offering tools or resources that simplify the deployment and management of AWS services.

===== SLIDE 42 =====
Managing a partner’s performance through standard Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) is essential to understand whether a partner is achieving their goals. Examples of standard KPIs include:
Plan Achievement:
The extent in which a partner organization meets the goals and objectives outlined in a strategic plan.
Sales Quota Achievement:
The percentage of quota achieved based on annual sales revenue.
Lead Conversion Rate:
The percentage of potential customers (leads) converted into paying customers. This is also measured by lead source: Channel-Originated (CO) and Fortra-Originated (FO)
Partner Win Rate:
The percentage of sales opportunities that are successfully closed as won.
Average Deal Size & Time to Close:
The average value of sales transactions over a specific period.
Pipeline Value:
The total potential revenue from all active sales opportunities within the partner’s pipeline.
YoY Growth:
Year-over-year growth in a partner’s sales.
Volume of Approved Deal Registrations:
Number of Partner-Originated deals registered by partner and approved by Fortra.
Marketing Development Funds (MDF) Results:
The ROI from a partner-led marketing activity that’s co-funded by Fortra. ROI can be measured by lead conversion rate, volume of sales-qualified leads (SQLs), etc.

Introduction
The
AWS Marketplace
is an online store where customers can discover, purchase, and deploy software applications and services that run on Amazon Web Services (AWS). It offers a wide range of solutions, including software for infrastructure management, security, data analytics, machine learning, networking, and more. These products are built to integrate seamlessly with AWS's cloud services.
An
AWS Marketplace Private Offer
allows AWS customers to access customized pricing and terms for security services and technologies provided by
Fortra
. These offers are typically negotiated directly between the AWS customer and a Fortra account executive or through a Fortra Channel Partner.
Fortra’s
Procurement Options:
Direct Sales
– Standard sales process through Fortra or through the Channel
Marketplace Private Offer (MPPO)
– Fortra creates a Private Offer with standard or custom pricing and terms for the customer to purchase products or services through the AWS Marketplace
Channel Partner Private Offer (CPPO)
– Fortra grants seller authorization to Channel Partners, enabling them to resell products or services through the AWS Marketplace
DSOR
– A distributor, acts as an intermediary between vendors (like software companies) and resellers or channel partners. The
DSOR
refers to the authoritative record or system where the distributor (Ingram Micro) keeps track of key transaction details
