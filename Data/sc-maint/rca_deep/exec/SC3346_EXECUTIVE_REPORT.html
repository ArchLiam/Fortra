The defect — in business terms

When a customer renews their maintenance, the renewal line was pricing at the wrong amount — a stale $60.64 (or $0) instead of the correct cost-of-living-adjusted renewal of $67.38 (last year’s net $62.48 × 7.85% COLA). The pricing math was always right; the value simply never committed to the line. Multiple prior attempts to patch it had failed because they all worked at the wrong layer.

Root cause — a selling-model mismatch (found via end-to-end debug)
  1. New-business maintenance is sold as a One-Time product, so the asset the customer owns is One-Time — not a renewable subscription.
  2. The platform’s renewal therefore tags that asset as “No Change” / quantity 0 — it never enters the COLA renewal-pricing path (which only fires on a Renew action).
  3. The legacy design then deleted that owned-asset line and substituted a separate “Renewal Maintenance” line that has no asset to price against — so it froze at the wrong value or $0.
  4. The fix: stop discarding the owned line — flip it to Renew and let the native engine apply the COLA uplift to last year’s net. No new pricing logic, no catalog change.
The fix — what was built & deployed

1 · Apex: RenewalMaintenanceFlip

Flips the owned-maintenance renewal line from No Change → Renew and sets the renew quantity. ~120 lines, bulk-safe, 4/4 unit tests pass. Deployed to UAT.

2 · Flow: Fortra_Create_Renewal_Quote

Wired so every renewal automatically runs flip → reprice:

initiate renewal → Flip Maintenance → Reprice → $67.38 committed
