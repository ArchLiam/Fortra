What DPP is

A maintenance product (support, updates, upgrades) has no standalone list price. Its price comes from the thing it attaches to — the license or subscription the customer is buying, or the maintenance asset they already own. DPP is the slice of the pricing procedure that computes those prices automatically.

Two branches, one engine

Which formula runs depends on whether this is new business or a renewal.

New-business maintenance selling new

A percentage of the license or subscription being sold — then the line's own discounts.

net = sourceBase × tier% then − partner% − discretionary%
  • Tiers — Standard 0.20 · Premier 0.30 · Professional 0.20 (from Maintenance_Rate__mdt)
  • Untagged line → defaults to Standard 0.20
  • No base → correctly $0, never a fabricated price

Renewal maintenance already owned

Carry the prior maintenance forward from the owned asset and apply a cost-of-living uplift.

first renewal (asset − priorPartner$ − priorDisc$) × (1+COLA) 2nd+ (RRM) asset × (1+COLA) — no re-discounting
  • First renewal nets the prior discounts once, then uplifts
  • RRM is single-component — the prior discount is not re-subtracted

The fix that closed the one real defect

A first-renewal maintenance line carrying a discretionary discount used to break on re-price: the engine mis-routed it into the new-business tier base (asset × 0.20) and produced a badly low net. A commit-layer override now re-computes the correct asset-based net and writes it over the procedure's value.

Mechanism: PartnerNetPricePosthook.buildRenewalColaCommitUpdate → COLAUpliftCalculator.computeFirstRenewalMaintenanceNet. All six renewal lines now match their formula to the cent (independently re-derived and adversarially verified). Companion line DKNN moved 58.58 → 65.09.

Read Net Unit Price, not COLA Calculated Price. The override corrects NetUnitPrice but intentionally leaves COLACalculatedPrice__c at the old value (e.g. 660.04). That field is a last-resort fallback only — the order and Workday take the corrected Net — so it's cosmetic on disk. Re-stamping it to match is a suggested tidy-up, not a defect.

Reading a maintenance line in the UI

Which field means what when you open a quote line to check it.

Net Unit Price
The answer. The authoritative derived price — this is what to verify.
Base Price
The procedure's intermediate. On fixed renewal lines it's overridden, so Base × COLA won't equal Net there — that's intended.
COLA Calculated Price
Ignore on renewals. Left intentionally stale by the fix; not used for order money.
List Price
0 is normal on a derived maintenance line. It's populated only on the source subscription (native currency).
Net Total Price
Net Unit × Qty. Occasionally blank on a renewal (a rollup quirk) — use Subtotal / Total Price, which are correct.
